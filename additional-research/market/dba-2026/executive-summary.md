## RED FLAGS IN JOB POSTINGS (WHAT TO AVOID)

### Warning Signs of Problematic Roles

🚩 **"Seeking DBA/Developer/DevOps hybrid"**
→ Three full-time roles disguised as one. Negotiate 50%+ higher salary or walk away.

🚩 **"On-call 24/7 with no rotation"**
→ Burnout guaranteed. Healthy teams have 3-4 person rotation.

🚩 **"Must have expert knowledge in: SQL Server, Oracle, PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB, Cassandra, Redis..."**
→ Unrealistic expectations. No one is expert in all platforms. Red flag for understanding of roles.

🚩 **"Junior DBA, 5+ years experience required"**
→ They want mid-level skills at junior-level pay. Negotiate or pass.

🚩 **"Salary range: $80K-$180K"**
→ Absurdly wide range = they have no idea what they're doing. Expect lowball offer.

🚩 **"Fast-paced startup environment, no legacy systems"**
→ Translation: No established processes, you'll build everything from scratch with minimal resources.

### Green Flags (Signs of Good Roles)

✅ **Clear tech stack listed** (e.g., "SQL Server 2019/2022, Azure SQL, Git, Terraform")
✅ **Specific responsibilities** rather than vague "manage databases"
✅ **Mentions team size** ("Join our team of 4 DBAs")
✅ **Training/conference budget mentioned**
✅ **Clear on-call rotation** ("1 week on-call per month, $500 stipend")
✅ **Reasonable salary range** ($120K-$145K vs $80K-$200K)

## FINAL THOUGHTS

As of March 2026, the source draft characterizes the database administrator role as active and evolving but fundamentally different than 2020.

**What's Changed**:
- Cloud-first (not cloud-optional)
- DevOps integration (not siloed)
- Multi-platform (not single-database expert)
- Strategic (not just operational)
- Security/compliance embedded (not afterthought)

**What Hasn't Changed**:
- SQL is still foundational
- Performance tuning still requires expertise
- Data integrity is still critical
- Communication skills still essential
- Continuous learning still mandatory

**The Opportunity**:
If you're a DBA feeling pressure from automation, **you're looking at it wrong**.

Automation is eliminating the boring parts of your job (backups, basic monitoring) and making room for the interesting parts (architecture, optimization, strategy).

The source draft reports: **+8-9% growth, strong salaries, high demand.**

**The catch**: You must evolve with the role. 2020 skills won't get 2026 jobs.

**The good news**: The path is clear (see the Career Path Optimization section), and the investment pays off quickly.

The database administrator profession isn't dying—**it's leveling up**.
